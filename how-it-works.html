How it works

Everyone knows what happens next.

AjoFam keeps traditional ajo and esusu exactly as it is. It just makes the rotation, the payments and the next turn visible to the whole group instead of to one person.

The loop

Contribute, wait, collect, repeat

Ajo runs in a loop. AjoFam’s only job is to show your group exactly where it is in that loop, at any moment.

  1. 01 Contribute
  2. 02 Wait your turn
  3. 03 Collect
  4. 04 Repeat

Nothing about ajo changes

The same amount, the same schedule, the same people, the same order. Money still moves the way your group already moves it — cash to the coordinator, a bank transfer, mobile money.

Only the record changes

Instead of one notebook that one person holds, there is one page that everyone holds. When a contribution comes in it is marked that day, and every member sees the same thing at the same time.

The process

From joining the group to closing the cycle

Eight steps, start to finish. Most of them are things your group already does — AjoFam just writes them down.

01

Create or join a group

Start a contribution group with people you trust, or join one that already exists. The coordinator sets the amount, the schedule and the number of seats.

02

Agree the contribution

Everyone sees how much to contribute, how often, and how many members are in the rotation — before the first payment, not after.

03

Draw the rotation

Seats are set once, at the start, and shown to everyone. Each member can see their position and how many turns away it is.

04

Make your contribution

Pay the coordinator however your group already does it — cash, transfer or mobile money. It is marked paid on the group page the same day.

05

Watch the record update

Paid, pending or missed, per member, per cycle. Nobody has to ask the coordinator, and the coordinator doesn’t have to answer.

06

Collect your turn

When your seat comes up, the group already knows. The pot goes out once every contribution for the cycle is in.

07

Handle a miss by the rules

If someone misses, the group’s own written rule applies — visible to everyone, decided in advance, applied the same way each time.

08

Close the cycle

Once every seat has collected, the group confirms every account is clear and decides together whether to run it again.

A worked example

Five members. One clear rotation.

Five friends contribute ₦10,000 every week. Each week one member receives the pooled ₦50,000. After five weeks everyone has collected once, and the cycle closes.

Weekly Ajo · week 3 ₦10,000 each

05 Fatima Paid Wednesday Paid
4 of 5 contributions in ₦50,000 expected
Read it in one line

Zainab collects ₦50,000 this week. Hauwa still owes. Aisha and Mariam have already collected but keep paying — that is how the loop stays whole.

The rule that catches people out

Collecting your turn does not end your payments. You contribute every week until the cycle finishes, whether your turn has come or not.

The maths, once

5 members × ₦10,000 = ₦50,000 a week. Over five weeks, everyone puts in ₦50,000 and takes out ₦50,000 — the value is the timing, not the total.

What everyone can see

The three things that keep a group accountable

01

Payment status

Which members have paid, which are still pending, and which have missed — for the current cycle and every cycle before it.

02

Next in line

The rotation order, fixed and visible, so there is never a question about whose turn it is to receive.

03

What a miss means

An overdue contribution is identified early and handled by the group’s own written rule — not lost in a busy chat.

When someone misses

The group sees the situation, not just a silence.

AjoFam keeps payment status visible so a pending or missed contribution is noticed early and handled the way the group agreed. The goal is not to shame anyone — it is to make the information clear enough for the group to respond fairly, and in time.
